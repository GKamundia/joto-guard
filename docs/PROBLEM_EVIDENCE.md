# Heat and outdoor work in Kenya: the evidence behind Joto Guard

Every figure below was checked against its source on 19 September 2026. Sources are listed at the end with links. Section 7 says what this evidence does not show.

## 1. Kenya

The Lancet Countdown's 2025 data sheet for Kenya [1] reports:

- **Labour lost to heat.** Heat exposure cost Kenya 1.1 billion potential labour hours in 2024, a record 51 hours per person and 186 % more than in 1990–1999. Agriculture accounted for 74 % of the loss.
- **Heatwaves.** People in Kenya were exposed to 11.5 heatwave days each in 2024, 250 % more than in 2020.
- **Heat-stress hours.** Compared with 1990–1999, people had 424 more hours in 2024 during which heat posed a moderate or higher heat-stress risk for moderate outdoor activity, also a record.

## 2. Heat around Juja

- **Kenya Met's thresholds.** The Kenya Meteorological Department defines a heatwave as three or more consecutive days above a locally set maximum temperature: 32 °C for Nairobi, 36 °C for Mombasa and 37 °C for Kisumu [2].
- **The hot season.** For 3 to 7 February 2026, Kenya Met forecast maxima of up to 33 °C for Nairobi and Kiambu [3].
- **Our record is the cool season.** The station record we have runs from 28 August to 15 September 2026, and its air temperature never exceeded 28.5 °C. The January to March hot season is not in our data.

## 3. Who works outdoors around Juja

- **Quarrying.** The extraction and sale of construction stone "has become one of the most significant economic activities in Juja", driven by booming real estate development, and many farmers have turned to quarrying [4, section 3.3]. The Ndarugu area of Juja has long been quarried for building stone [5]. Juja's machine-cut stone quarries supply construction in the Nairobi metropolitan area; a 2006 study counted 41 active quarries [6], and the quarries are still studied today [7].
- **Construction.** The same plan lists a well-developed housing and real estate sector among the municipality's strengths [4, section 4.0].
- **Farming.** Small-scale farming and quarrying are the main economic activities around the Ndarugu river [5]. The plan lists good arable land among Juja's strengths [4].
- **Plantation work next door.** Del Monte Kenya's pineapple plantation in neighbouring Thika covers more than 4,200 ha and employs over 7,700 workers, who plant and harvest by hand [8].
- **Informal work.** The plan also lists manufacturing and *jua kali* (informal artisan) work among the area's activities [4, section 4.0].

## 4. Kenyan workers and heat

- **Farmers in Siaya.** 48 subsistence farmers wore research-grade sensors while outdoor WBGT was measured. Outdoor WBGT peaked between 14:00 and 15:00, and women showed greater physiological strain than men [9]. A 24-month follow-up study is measuring how heat reduces their work capacity [10].
- **Community health workers.** In Kenya they deliver about half of health services, and 80 % of that work means being outside and moving between homes [11]. In Mombasa and Tana River counties they describe heat-related illness and pressure to keep working [11, 12].
- **Smallholder farmers in East Africa.** Projected WBGT during the busiest farming periods would require up to 75 % rest in each working hour by 2050 in parts of eastern and coastal Kenya in February and March. Eastern and northern Kenya are also exposed in August and September [13].

## 5. Beyond Juja: Kisumu and Mombasa

The 3D-PAWS FEWS NET portal that serves Conduit@Empathy1 also lists a 3D-PAWS station at Kisumu Airport, and stations at KALRO Mtwapa in Kilifi, just north of Mombasa, and at KALRO Matuga in Kwale [14]. Given download access, Joto Guard can run on any station that reports the same variables, once that station has its own light calibration and forecast correction.

## 6. What this means for Joto Guard

Much of the work around Juja is heavy and done outdoors: quarrying and cutting building stone, construction, and farming. Kenya Met's advisories give temperatures for whole counties with general advice; they do not say when a given kind of work should slow down. Joto Guard is designed to do that, hour by hour and by type of work, for the place the station stands.

## 7. Limits of this evidence

- No published study has measured heat stress in Juja itself. Sections 1 and 4 are national or from other counties.
- The Kenya Met items are news reports of its forecasts and definitions, not Kenya Met publications.
- The quarry count in [6] is from 2006. The 2023 county plan [4] confirms quarrying is still one of Juja's most significant activities, but not how many people it employs.
- Our own station record covers only three cool-season weeks.
